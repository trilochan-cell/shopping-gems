## Event-by-event breakdown

### New Year Sale (January)

The year's opening event, built on winter-clearance fashion and early tech deals. Discount depth is strong (four stars) but the real edge is *stackability*: clearance markdowns combine with store coupons and platform codes for the famous triple stack. Shop jackets, boots, knitwear, earbuds, and smart-home starters. Skip: newly launched flagship phones, which are almost always excluded. Full tactics in the [New Year Sale guide](/posts/aliexpress-new-year-sale/).

### Anniversary Sale (late March)

AliExpress's birthday party — and pound for pound the best spring shopping event on the internet. Sitewide coupons run deep, seller participation is near-total, and the category spread is the widest outside 11.11. This is your moment for spring wardrobe refreshes, home organization hauls, mid-range electronics, and beauty stock-ups. If you only shop two AliExpress events a year, make them this one and 11.11.

### Summer Sale (June)

Built for heat: swimwear, sunglasses, outdoor and camping gear, fans and cooling gadgets, and travel accessories. Discounts peak in seasonal categories while tech runs merely warm — save big electronics for November. June is also when sellers clear spring inventory, so fashion basics (tees, shorts, sandals) hit their yearly lows. Check our [New Year stacking mechanics](/posts/aliexpress-new-year-sale/) — the coupon system works identically here.

### Back to School (August)

The smallest major event, but a quiet gem for its niche: stationery, backpacks, dorm organization, desk lamps, budget tablets, and kids' wear. Discount depth is modest, so don't reshape your year around it — but if you have students to equip, August beats September prices by 15–25% on exactly these items. Combine with the [Amazon calendar](/posts/upcoming-amazon-sales-2026/) if you need first-party brands with local warranty.

### 11.11 Global Shopping Festival (November 11)

The undisputed king. Born as Singles' Day, 11.11 is now the largest shopping event on earth by volume, and AliExpress goes all-in: the deepest platform coupons of the year, stackable store codes, coin bonuses, midnight Super Deals, and brand-day takeovers. Strategy: save *all* big-ticket purchases — phones, tablets, laptops, e-bikes, robot vacuums — for this single week. Build wishlists from October, claim coupons during the late-October warm-up, and check out high-priority items at midnight when Super Deals go live, because flagship listings sell through within hours.

### Black Friday / Cyber Monday (late November)

Roughly two weeks after 11.11, with a more Western flavor: TVs, laptops, gaming gear, and global brands take center stage, and many sellers simply extend their 11.11 pricing. Rule of thumb: if an item didn't sell out on 11.11, it will likely be the same price or a touch cheaper now; if it *did* sell out, Black Friday restocks are your second chance. Cross-check Amazon's [Black Friday week](/posts/upcoming-amazon-sales-2026/) before committing — the two platforms undercut each other all week.

### Christmas Sale (December)

Gifts, toys, festive decor, and party supplies at 20–40% off, with shipping-speed filters mattering more than raw discount depth. December trap: standard shipping may not arrive before the holidays, so pay for tracked or premium shipping on anything date-critical, or buy early in the event window. ## Which sales actually deserve your calendar?

Score every event on two axes: *depth* (how big the coupons are) and *breadth* (how many categories participate). 11.11 and the Anniversary Sale max out both — these are the two "move your purchase" events. New Year, Summer, and Black Friday are deep but narrower — shop them when they match your category. Back to School and Christmas are situational: unbeatable inside their niche, ignorable outside it.

The golden rule: never buy a $100+ item within three weeks *before* a four-star-or-better event in its category. That single habit saves more money than any coupon code.

## The universal sale playbook (works at every event)

1. **Wishlist 2–4 weeks early.** Your wishlist is your price-history tracker — it exposes fake "discounts" instantly.
2. **Claim during warm-up.** Store coupons are quantity-limited; platform codes usually aren't. Twenty minutes of claiming beats twenty dollars of overpaying.
3. **Stack in order:** store coupon → platform code → coins → payment promo. Practice the sequence on a small order before 11.11.
4. **Vet sellers once, buy confidently.** 95%+ feedback, recent photo reviews, tracked shipping over $30. Full checklist in our [New Year guide](/posts/aliexpress-new-year-sale/).
5. **Mind the customs threshold.** Split parcels to stay under your local duty-free limit, especially on 11.11 mega-hauls.
6. **Confirm receipt only after testing.** Electronics get tested before you click "received" — chargebacks after confirmation are uphill battles.

## Price tracking: your unfair advantage

The strikethrough "original price" is marketing; your wishlist is truth. For any planned purchase over $50, watch the listing for two weeks pre-sale and screenshot the price. During the event, compute the *stacked* total (coupons + codes + coins) rather than admiring the banner discount. Shoppers who track beat shoppers who browse by 10–20% on identical carts — consistently, every event, every year. Extend the habit across platforms: the same screenshot discipline applied to our [Amazon calendar](/posts/upcoming-amazon-sales-2026/) turns cross-platform comparison from guesswork into arithmetic. And keep the screenshots for a month post-delivery — if a price drops further inside the dispute window, evidence of the higher charge strengthens adjustment requests. Tracking is a fifteen-minute habit with a permanent payoff curve: each event's history makes the next event's judgments faster and sharper, until sale-week decisions become nearly automatic.

## Frequently skipped, frequently regretted

- **Payment promos.** An extra 5–10% for using a specific card or wallet, hiding in plain sight on the payments page.
- **Follower coupons.** Following a store often unlocks exclusive codes before public ones drop.
- **Coin redemptions.** Trivial per item, meaningful per haul — and they stack with everything.
- **Post-sale reviews with photos.** They earn coins that fund your *next* event's discounts.
- **The ten-minute debrief.** After every event, note what worked (coupon tiers hit, fastest sellers, what sold out first) in a running log. That file compounds into personal sale intelligence no guide can replicate — by your third event, you'll anticipate drops and sellouts like a veteran.

## Coupon types glossary (collect them all)

- **Store coupons:** seller-issued, thresholds per store, quantity-limited. The scarcest and most valuable.
- **Platform promo codes:** sitewide, usually unlimited, the backbone of every stack.
- **Super Deal prices:** time-boxed doorbusters, no code needed, sell out fast.
- **Coin redemptions:** loyalty micro-discounts on eligible items; stack with everything.
- **Payment promos:** card/wallet discounts applied last at checkout.
- **Follower coupons:** store-follow exclusives, often better than public codes.
- **Bundle offers:** automatic extra-off for multi-item or multi-quantity buys.
- **New-buyer coupons:** first-order perks — worth routing a small first purchase through a new account where legitimate.

Knowing all eight means never leaving money in a layer you didn't know existed.

## The 11.11 war-room plan (save this for October)

11.11 deserves its own operation plan because scale breaks casual habits. **Late October:** finalize wishlists, claim every warm-up coupon, verify payment promos, and set a per-category budget cap (11.11's danger is overbuying, not overpaying). **November 1–10:** watch pre-sale prices daily; some sellers offer deposit pre-orders locking lower totals. **November 11, 00:00:** check out Super Deal priorities first, then the main cart, then sleep. **November 11–13:** mop-up round — flash coupons, restocked sizes, forgotten fillers. **Aftermath:** track parcels weekly, test electronics on arrival, and leave photo reviews that fund next year's coins. Shoppers who run this plan spend 15–25% less than same-cart impulse buyers — the event rewards process, not speed.

## What to never buy on AliExpress (any sale)

Balance matters: some categories are structurally bad buys. **Safety-critical gear** (helmets, climbing hardware, car seats) where certification fraud has real consequences. **High-capacity storage** from unknown brands (fake-capacity flash is endemic — stick to brand flagships). **Anything needing local warranty service** (large appliances with no service network). **Perishables and supplements** without sealed, verifiable sourcing. And **"too good" luxury** — a $40 "designer" bag is a $40 bag; buy it for what it is, never for the logo. Everything else is fair game with proper seller vetting from our [New Year guide](/posts/aliexpress-new-year-sale/).

## Sale math: computing your real discount (with examples)

Banner percentages lie; stacked totals tell the truth. The formula: **real discount = 1 − (final stacked total ÷ pre-sale baseline)**. The baseline is your wishlist-tracked price, never the strikethrough. Worked examples from typical events:

**Example A — Anniversary tech haul:** earbuds baseline $45, event price $32. Store coupon −$4 (over $30 tier) → $28. Platform code −$3 (over $29 tier across combined cart) → $25. Coins −$0.80 → $24.20. Card promo 5% → ~$23. Real discount: 1 − (23 ÷ 45) = **49%** — nearly double the 29% banner.

**Example B — Summer fashion bundle:** three items baseline $60 total, event total $44. One store coupon −$5 → $39. Platform code needs $49, so add a $7 filler (socks you actually need) → $46 subtotal, −$6 code → $40 for four items versus $60 baseline = **33%**, plus a free pair of socks.

**Example C — 11.11 flagship phone:** baseline $499, event $429. Store coupon −$30 → $399. Platform code −$25 (high-tier 11.11 codes) → $374. Coins −$3 → $371. Payment promo −$15 → **$356 final, 29% real discount** on a product that "never goes on sale."

**The spreadsheet habit:** one row per planned item (baseline, event price, coupons, codes, final), one column for the event name. Fifteen minutes per event, and you'll never wonder whether a deal is real again. Skip any item whose real discount lands under 15% — normal price fluctuation covers that within weeks, and the [deals hub](/deals) will surface a better window soon.
