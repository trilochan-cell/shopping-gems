For a business to grow, the most important thing is to figure out your audience — and then meet them where they already search, scroll, and shop. Google Ads, Meta Ads, and TikTok Ads all promise the best ROI, but they win in completely different situations, measure success differently, and punish different mistakes. This guide gives you an honest, numbers-first comparison: what each platform actually costs, where each one wins, the budget frameworks that work at each business stage, and a decision process for picking your starting channel.

## The short answer

| Platform | Superpower | Biggest weakness | Start here if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Ads | Highest purchase intent | Highest click costs | People search for what you sell |
| Meta Ads | Creative testing at scale | Creative fatigue in weeks | You need to create demand |
| TikTok Ads | Cheapest reach | Demands native video | Your product is visual + impulse-friendly |

- **Selling something people search for** (plumbers, software, "buy X")? Start with Google.
- **Selling something people discover** (fashion, gadgets, DTC brands)? Start with Meta or TikTok.
- **Small budget, big ambition?** TikTok gives the most impressions per dollar while you learn what resonates.

The rest of this guide explains *why* — and what to do in weeks one through four on whichever you pick.

## Google Ads: capture existing demand

Google's superpower is intent. Someone typing "buy noise-cancelling earbuds under $100" has essentially pre-qualified themselves; your job is to be the best answer, not to persuade them they have a problem. That is why Search campaigns convert at 2–4% routinely while social prospecting celebrates at half that.

**Where the money goes.** Brand and high-intent exact-match campaigns first — these are your profit engine. Then Performance Max or broad match for discovery, with tight conversion tracking so the algorithm learns what a buyer looks like. Shopping/free-listing surfaces matter enormously for e-commerce: your product feed quality (titles, images, GTINs) directly moves click-through and cost.

**What it costs.** Competitive keywords run $2–$10+ per click in finance, legal, SaaS, and home services; long-tail e-commerce terms often land under $1. Benchmarks vary wildly by niche, so treat any "average CPC" you read as entertainment — your auction is the only one that matters. Budget rule: monthly spend should cover at least 10–15 clicks per day on your core terms, or the learning never stabilizes.

**Where Google loses.** Pure discovery. Nobody searches for products they don't know exist, so new categories, novel gadgets, and lifestyle brands stall on Search alone. Google is also the least forgiving of weak landing pages — you pay per click regardless, so a slow or confusing page just burns money faster.

## Meta Ads: manufacture demand at scale

Facebook and Instagram excel at interest-based discovery: finding buyers who never searched, via creative that interrupts the scroll. The machine is a testing engine — broad audiences plus 5–10 creative variants, letting delivery find pockets of buyers you'd never have targeted manually. Advertisers who embrace creative volume (new variants weekly) consistently beat those who "find one winner and rest."

**Where the money goes.** Advantage+/broad prospecting for discovery, retargeting for the warm middle (site visitors, video viewers, engagers), and retention to existing customers where margins allow. UGC-style creative — real people, real lighting, captions burned in — outperforms studio polish in most DTC categories.

**What it costs.** CPMs typically land $8–$25 depending on audience, season (Q4 costs roughly double), and placement. Creative fatigue is the tax: winning ads decay in 2–4 weeks as frequency climbs, so budget a steady pipeline of new images and short videos. Accounts die more often from creative starvation than from targeting mistakes.

**Where Meta loses.** High-consideration B2B, emergency services, and anything where the buyer needs the product *now* — intent beats interruption there. iOS-era attribution is also foggier than Google's click-based world, so you must triangulate with first-party data (covered below).

## TikTok Ads: cheapest reach, native or nothing

TikTok offers the lowest CPMs of the three — often $3–$8 — and an algorithm that can scale a $30/day test into six figures of views overnight. The catch is absolute: lo-fi, native-style vertical video wins; polished TV-style creative flops, sometimes spectacularly. Hook in the first two seconds, subtitles on (most viewing is muted), trending sounds where licensable, and a creator-style face to camera.

**Where the money goes.** Spark Ads on organic posts that already show traction (validate creative free, then pay to scale it), interest/behavior prospecting for cold reach, and retargeting for the consideration tail. TikTok Shop integration, where available, collapses the funnel beautifully for impulse-friendly products under $50 — exactly the kind of finds deal blogs like our [deals hub](/deals) feature daily.

**What it costs.** Beyond cheap CPMs, production is the hidden bargain: a smartphone, decent window light, and a trending format beat a $5k studio shoot. The real cost is *cadence* — TikTok rewards accounts that post and test constantly, so assign someone (or some hours) to creative every week.

**Where TikTok loses.** Anything requiring trust before purchase (finance, health, high-ticket B2B), audiences over 45 (present but thinner), and products that can't be demonstrated visually in nine seconds.

## Head-to-head: costs, intent, and measurement

| Dimension | Google | Meta | TikTok |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical CPC range | $1–$10+ | $0.50–$3 | $0.30–$1.50 |
| Typical CPM range | $20–$60+ (display) | $8–$25 | $3–$8 |
| Buyer intent | Highest | Medium | Lowest (highest discovery) |
| Attribution clarity | High (click-based) | Medium (modeled) | Medium-low (view-heavy) |
| Creative half-life | Long (text/search) | 2–4 weeks | 1–3 weeks |
| Learning budget | Highest | Medium | Lowest |

Ranges are directional — your niche, geography, and season move them more than any benchmark article can. Use them to set expectations, not budgets; set budgets from your own break-even math (below).

## Budget frameworks by stage

**Stage 1 — validation ($20–50/day, one channel).** One campaign, one clear offer, one conversion event. Goal isn't profit; it's answering "can paid traffic buy customers near my target CPA?" Kill threshold: 2x target CPA after ~50 clicks with zero conversions.

**Stage 2 — proving ($100–300/day, one to two channels).** Split winning Stage-1 creative into 3–5 variants; open retargeting; introduce the second channel with a 70/20/10 split (70% proven, 20% new test, 10% reserve to scale winners). Optimize weekly to ROAS, not daily to vibes.

**Stage 3 — scaling ($500+/day, multi-channel).** Dedicated creative production (the bottleneck is always creative), structured testing roadmaps, and blended MER (marketing efficiency ratio) as the north-star metric alongside in-platform ROAS. At this stage, incrementality testing (geo holdouts, on/off pulses) separates real growth from double-counted conversions.

**The break-even formula.** Know these three numbers before spending a dollar: average order value, gross margin, and repeat-purchase rate. Break-even CPA ≈ AOV × margin × (1 + repeat contribution). If your allowable CPA is $25 and a channel delivers at $40 after two weeks of honest optimization, the channel isn't broken — the offer, page, or targeting is. Fix inputs before blaming platforms.

## Measurement: trust, but triangulate

No single dashboard tells the truth anymore. Run three lenses: in-platform reporting (directional, optimistic), first-party analytics with UTM discipline (your source of truth for behavior), and a blended business metric — MER (total revenue ÷ total ad spend) or new-customer CPA. When platform ROAS says 5x and MER says 2x, believe MER and investigate overlap: retargeting poaching organic demand is the usual suspect. Set conversion windows deliberately (shorter for low-consideration, longer for high), and give every test a pre-committed kill/scale rule so Tuesdays don't become emotional trading days.

## The creative pipeline (the actual job)

Across all three platforms, creative — not targeting, not bidding — is now the primary lever. Build a weekly rhythm: mine comments, reviews, and support tickets for hooks; ship 3–5 new variants per active channel; brief with a one-page template (hook, proof, offer, CTA); kill losers at statistical relevance, not boredom; bank winners into an "evergreen" file for seasonal rescaling. One person spending half a day a week on this beats any audience hack.

## Mistakes that burn budgets

- **No conversion tracking** (or tracking pageviews as "conversions") — the algorithms optimize toward exactly what you measure.
- **Testing three platforms with one platform's budget** — $20/day split three ways learns nothing anywhere.
- **Judging at day three.** Two weeks or ~50 conversions per ad set before verdicts.
- **Recycled creative everywhere.** One generic video underperforms natively-shot creative on every platform.
- **Scaling spend before fixing the page.** A 1%-converting page at 10x spend is just a bigger bonfire; fix to baseline first.
- **Ignoring post-click.** Slow pages, forced accounts, and surprise shipping murder ROAS that the ads earned.

## The 60-second decision process

Answer in order: (1) Do people search for this with intent? Yes → start Google. (2) Is it visual, impulse-friendly, under ~$50? Yes → start TikTok. (3) Otherwise → start Meta. Prove economics on one channel for a month, then expand in the order above. Revisit quarterly — auctions, audiences, and your own margins all drift.

## Landing pages: where ad money is actually made

Traffic is bought; conversions are built on the page. Before scaling any channel, fix these fundamentals: **message match** (headline repeats the ad's promise within three seconds), **one page, one job** (single CTA above the fold — navigation links are leaks), **proof near the claim** (reviews, numbers, logos adjacent to assertions, not buried below), **friction audit** (guest checkout, autofill-friendly forms, no surprise shipping), and **speed** (each second of load time costs roughly 7% of conversions — compress images, defer non-critical scripts). A page converting at 3% instead of 1% triples every channel's ROAS overnight; no audience tweak competes with that multiplier.

## Attribution setup that survives iOS privacy

**UTM discipline first:** every ad URL tagged with source, medium, campaign, and content — non-negotiable, free, and the foundation of all first-party analysis. **Platform integrations second:** Conversions API (Meta), Enhanced Conversions (Google), and Events API (TikTok) send server-side events that recover 10–30% of conversions browsers now block. **Blended metrics third:** track MER (total revenue ÷ total ad spend) and new-customer CPA weekly; when in-platform ROAS and MER diverge, trust MER. **Holdouts fourth (quarterly):** pause one channel geo-region for two weeks and measure the business-wide delta — the only honest incrementality read. This stack takes a weekend to build and pays for itself the first time it stops you from scaling a mirage.

## The scaling SOP: from winner to workhorse

Found an ad set beating target CPA? Scale with procedure, not enthusiasm: **horizontal first** (duplicate into adjacent audiences with 70% of the original budget — fresh learning, contained risk), **vertical second** (raise budgets 20–30% every 48–72 hours; doubling overnight resets learning and torches efficiency), **creative refresh in parallel** (every scale-up accelerates fatigue — ship 2–3 variants the same week you scale), and **margin guardrails** (pre-set CPA ceilings per ad set that auto-pause breaches; humans hesitate, rules don't). Document each scale in a one-line log (date, change, result) — six months of logs is a playbook no course can sell you.

## DIY, freelancer, or agency: who should run this?

**DIY** while spend is under ~$3k/month — nobody learns your business faster than you, and the skills compound. **Freelancer** from ~$3–15k/month when creative production or campaign ops exceed your hours; hire for a *system* (briefing, testing cadence, reporting), not vibes, and keep account ownership always. **Agency** above that or for specialized needs (feed management, international expansion, creative studios) — but demand channel-level P&L transparency and a 90-day exit clause; performance should be the retainer's justification, renewed quarterly. Whatever the model, never outsource the break-even math or the kill/scale rules — those are ownership decisions.

## Three budgets, three playbooks (illustrative)

**Local service business, $1,500/month:** 80% Google Search on exact-match service + geo terms, 20% Meta retargeting of site visitors with review-led creative. Expect 15–30 qualified calls monthly at $50–100 each; the website's click-to-call button and five-star badges do half the converting. Kill rule: any keyword over $120 spend with zero calls gets paused, no sentimentality.

**DTC gadget brand, $6,000/month:** 50% TikTok (Spark Ads on proven organics + interest prospecting), 30% Meta (broad prospecting with weekly creative drops), 20% Google (brand + shopping for capture). Target blended MER above 3.0; TikTok prospecting will show the worst last-click ROAS and the best new-customer rate — judge it on the latter. Creative cadence: five new variants weekly minimum, or fatigue eats the prospecting lines within a month.

**B2B SaaS trial, $15,000/month:** 60% Google (high-intent categories, competitor conquest capped at 15%, Performance Max for expansion), 25% Meta (lead-gen forms + retargeting with case-study creative), 15% TikTok testing (founder-led explainers — cheap experiments, occasional breakouts). North star: payback period under 9 months on new-customer CAC, measured in the CRM, not the ad dashboards. Quarterly geo-holdout validates incrementality before any budget doubles.

These are starting allocations, not laws — your break-even math and two weeks of data always outrank any template, including this one.

## Glossary: the six metrics that matter

**CPC** (cost per click) — what each visitor costs; compare against your conversion rate, never in isolation. **CPM** (cost per thousand impressions) — the price of attention; TikTok cheapest, Google display dearest. **CVR** (conversion rate) — visitors who complete the goal; the multiplier that makes or breaks every channel. **CPA** (cost per acquisition) — spend divided by conversions; keep under your break-even number from the formula above. **ROAS** (return on ad spend) — revenue per dollar, platform-reported and usually optimistic. **MER** (marketing efficiency ratio) — total business revenue per total ad dollar, the metric that keeps everyone honest. Learn these six cold and every dashboard, agency report, and benchmark article becomes readable — which is precisely why this glossary sits near the end: you now have the context to use it.

Pick your lane, fund the learning, measure honestly — ROI follows process, not platforms.
