## AliExpress New Year Sale 2026: phases and key dates

The sale runs in three phases. Each phase has a job — and shoppers who treat warm-up as seriously as checkout day save the most.

| Phase | When | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Early January | Add to cart, claim every store coupon and platform code |
| Main sale | Mid-January (~7 days) | Check out with stacked codes, hit midnight Super Deals |
| Last chance | Late January (2–3 days) | Use leftover coupons before they expire |

Dates shift by a few days each year, so treat early-to-mid January as the window and watch the AliExpress homepage countdown. We update this page the moment dates are confirmed — and our [deals hub](/deals) tracks every event in one place.

**Why warm-up matters more than sale day.** Store coupons are limited in quantity and the good ones (high discount, low minimum spend) get claimed fast. Platform promo codes are usually unlimited, but store-level codes are not. Spend 20 minutes during warm-up claiming everything from stores you might buy from — unclaimed coupons are the number-one reason shoppers overpay.

## What to buy: category-by-category deal picks

Not every category discounts equally. Here is where the real money is.

### Tech and gadgets (the headliner)

Earbuds, smartwatches, Bluetooth speakers, phone accessories, LED lighting, and small smart-home gadgets routinely drop 30–50%. Flagship-adjacent phones from Chinese brands also see meaningful cuts, though the very newest launches are usually excluded. Prioritize listings marked with high order counts and recent reviews that mention the sale firmware or version — old-stock traps are common in tech.

### Fashion and winter clearance

January is winter-clearance season, and it overlaps the sale beautifully: jackets, hoodies, boots, and knitwear get a clearance markdown *plus* store coupons *plus* platform codes. That triple stack is the best clothing math of the year. Check size charts against your measurements (Asian sizing runs 1–2 sizes small) and filter for sellers with real customer photos in reviews.

### Home, kitchen, and organization

LED strips, storage organizers, kitchen gadgets, humidifiers, and bedding see 25–40% drops with generous free shipping, since these ship light. This is the category where "add five small things from one store" pays off — combined orders cross store-coupon minimums you would never hit with a single item.

### Beauty and personal care

Korean and Chinese skincare, electric toothbrushes, hair tools, and fragrance minis discount well. Stick to sealed, high-volume listings and check expiry-date mentions in reviews for anything perishable. Counterfeit risk is highest here, so seller vetting (covered below) matters double.

### Toys and kids

Birthday and holiday stocking months ahead at nearly half price. Building blocks, RC cars, plush, and educational tablets for kids are classic January steals — buy for the whole year in one order and let store-coupon thresholds do the rest.

## Coupon stacking masterclass: the exact order

Stacking is the whole game. Here are the four layers and the order to apply them.

1. **Store coupons.** Issued by individual sellers ("$2 off $20", "$5 off $49"). Claim during warm-up; they auto-apply at checkout when your subtotal from that store qualifies. Pro move: bundle multiple items from the *same* store to cross the threshold.
2. **Platform promo codes.** Sitewide AliExpress codes ("$3 off $29", "$9 off $79"). Enter at checkout or auto-apply during the main sale. These stack *on top of* store coupons.
3. **Coins.** Earned from daily check-ins and games, redeemable for small additional discounts on eligible items. Pocket change per item, but real money across a big haul.
4. **Payment discounts.** Card and wallet promos (extra 5–10% with specific payment methods) apply last. Check the payments page during the sale — these rotate.

### A worked example

A $64 tech order: store coupon takes $8 off (qualifying $59+ tier) → $56. Platform code takes $6 off (qualifying $49+ tier) → $50. Coins shave $1.20 → $48.80. A 5% card promo → ~$46.40 final. That is 27% off a price that was *already* a sale price — and it required zero luck, just the right order of operations.

> Tip: always test the stack in the cart *before* the final day. If a code fails its minimum, add a filler item (cables, socks, kitchen brushes) rather than abandoning the coupon.

## How to vet sellers (5-minute check)

A cheap price from a bad seller is the most expensive purchase you will make. Run this check on anything over $20:

- **Feedback score 95%+** with at least a few hundred transactions. Below 93% is a walk-away.
- **Recent reviews with photos.** Ignore five-star walls with no images; look for reviews from the last 60 days mentioning delivery time and item condition.
- **"Top rated" and high order counts** on the exact listing, not just the store.
- **Clear warranty/return terms** in the listing — "Free returns" badges matter for electronics and sized clothing.
- **Responsive seller.** Message a question before buying; no reply within 24 hours predicts no support after buying.

## Shipping, delivery, and customs: the honest version

**Shipping tiers.** Standard AliExpress shipping to most regions takes 7–20 days during sales (volume slows everything). Premium or express options cut that to 3–7 days for a fee — worth it for gifts with deadlines, pointless for stock-up hauls. Consolidated shipping (multiple items arriving together) often appears at checkout for single-store orders.

**Customs and import tax.** This is the silent budget killer. Many countries have a duty-free threshold (often around $150, but it varies widely) — keep individual parcels under your local limit or split big orders across shipments. If an order lands near the threshold, the tax can erase the entire discount. When in doubt, split.

**The tracking rule.** Never buy an item over $30 without tracked shipping. During sale chaos, untracked parcels are the ones that "arrive" nowhere, and dispute windows are harder to win without tracking evidence.

## Mistakes that cost real money

- **Checking out during warm-up.** Prices drop further when the main sale goes live. Warm-up is for cart-building and coupon-claiming, never for paying.
- **Ignoring shipping fees.** A $12 gadget with $8 shipping is a $20 gadget. Sort by "price + shipping" mentally, always.
- **Chasing the lowest price from sub-93% sellers.** You will pay the difference in disputes, replacements, and time.
- **Forgetting coins and payment promos.** They feel trivial per item and add up to 5–10% across a haul.
- **Buying "original price" theater.** Compare against your wishlist history and the [full 2026 calendar](/posts/aliexpress-sales-in-2026/), not the strikethrough number.
- **One giant parcel over the customs threshold.** Split orders to stay under your local duty-free limit.

**Price-match awareness:** keep the [Amazon calendar](/posts/upcoming-amazon-sales-2026/) open in a second tab for anything over $50 — when Amazon's event price undercuts your stacked AliExpress total (common on branded electronics with local warranty), split the cart without hesitation. Platform loyalty is a luxury; stacked totals are the truth.

## Brand spotlight: stores worth following year-round

A handful of seller archetypes consistently outperform during the New Year Sale. **Official brand stores** (Anker-type accessories, Roborock-type appliances, Lenovo-type tech) run the steepest store coupons because they have margin to play with — follow them in December for early codes. **High-volume fashion houses** with 100k+ followers drop follower-exclusive coupons that beat public codes by 2–5%. **Niche gadget labs** (LED, smart home, EDC gear) compete on novelty and often bundle freebies (extra cables, mounts) instead of deeper discounts — better value than it looks. And **beauty flagships** with authenticity guarantees are the only skincare sellers worth your money. Build a followed-store list of 10–15 across these archetypes and your warm-up coupon haul will cover nearly any cart.

## Ranking payment methods for the sale

Not all ways to pay are equal during the event. **Credit cards with event tie-ups** usually win outright: 5–10% instant discounts that stack after coupons and coins. **Debit cards** from partner banks typically qualify for smaller instant discounts (5–8%) with lower caps — fine for sub-$50 orders. **Digital wallets** occasionally run their own promos (cashback rather than instant off), which is slower money but real. **Cash on delivery**, where available, almost never qualifies for promos — avoid it during sales. The hierarchy: check the event payments page on day one, pick the highest-percentage method whose cap fits your cart size, and set it as default before the midnight rush so checkout takes seconds.

## Disputes and refunds: winning when things go wrong

Even vetted sellers ship duds, and sale volume multiplies errors. Your protection is procedural. **Document on arrival:** unbox electronics on video, photograph packaging damage before discarding anything, and test every function within 24 hours. **Dispute windows are real deadlines** — most close 15 days after auto-confirmation, and "I'll deal with it later" forfeits your leverage. **Escalate with evidence:** AliExpress's dispute team sides with timestamped photos, tracking records, and message logs showing the seller unresponsive or contradictory. Message the seller first (many refunds settle in one message), escalate to platform mediation the moment promises stall, and never confirm receipt on a defective item — confirmation ends most claims instantly. Shoppers who follow this process win the overwhelming majority of legitimate disputes; shoppers who confirm-and-complain lose nearly all of them.
